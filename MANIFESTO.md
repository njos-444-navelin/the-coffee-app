# The Coffee App — Manifesto

The open, community-owned source of truth for coffee, built around personal taste. Every product decision is measured against this document. Proposals that conflict with it change; the document changes only through the process at the end.

## Who it is for, and what it does

Tailored with specialty coffee standards in mind, but made to be useful for coffee lovers with any and all preferences.

We intend to cover everything related to roasted coffee: discovering coffee shops, roasters, coffees, varieties, and processing methods; documenting one's own coffee journey; sharing experiences and brew recipes; and gathering coffee knowledge of every kind. The goal is to enhance each person's coffee journey, help them navigate it, let them share it, and build a connected community around it.

## Principles

### 1. Taste is personal, not ranked
- No global "best" lists or leaderboards. We recommend what a person is likely to enjoy, not what is best.
- Suggestions outside someone's comfort zone are always labeled as experiments.
- Reviews from people with similar taste are surfaced first.

### 2. Trust is the product
- We never sell ranking, placement, or visibility. No promoted pins, no sponsored results, ever.
- Sponsorships and partnerships are disclosed where their effect is seen.
- Recommendations are never influenced by who pays us.

### 3. The community owns the data
- Reference data (roasters, coffees, cafés, farms, aggregate ratings) is open data under [ODbL](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/).
- Wiki content is [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).
- Personal data (accounts, diaries, taste profiles) is never published or sold.
- Reviews are personal expression: visible and credited in the app, excluded from open exports. Aggregates may be published.
- Every record carries provenance: who added it, from where, under which license. License-incompatible data is not imported.
- Users can export their own data at any time.

### 4. Structured over freeform
- Every logged experience links to a canonical entity: coffee, roaster, or café.
- Less linked data beats more unlinked data.
- Taste descriptors have two layers: a curated canonical vocabulary that gives structure and explainability, and free descriptors users may add. Nothing a user says is thrown away: free descriptors are kept, searchable, and used as signal for matching people and coffees, and are promoted or mapped to canonical ones as usage grows.

### 5. Open source, forever
- Code is [AGPL-3.0](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html).
- No Contributor License Agreement, ever. Contributors keep copyright; sign-off is via [DCO](https://developercertificate.org/).
- No open core. Core features are never held back for a paid tier.

### 6. The name belongs to the project
"The Coffee App" and its marks are held by the project for the community. The project may earn money under its name; nobody else may without its consent.

### 7. Money serves the project
- Acceptable revenue: honest tools for roasters and cafés, transparent commerce, donations, grants.
- Never: advertising, attention, engagement, or sale of data.
- No equity investors. Money is never accepted in exchange for ownership or control.
- Finances are public.
- If the project ever produces a surplus, the intent is to share it with contributors, including non-code contributors, through a transparent published process. Legal form and process are deferred to a future `GOVERNANCE.md`.

### 8. Reputation reflects usefulness, not activity
- Reputation grows when contributions are used, confirmed, and found helpful by others.
- Never from streaks, logins, check-in counts, or volume for its own sake.
- Bulk and everyday contributors earn it the same way and are credited on the records they added.

### 9. Privacy by default
- Diaries are private unless an entry is shared.
- Location helps find coffee nearby; it never tracks people.
- Personal data is analyzed only to serve the person it belongs to.
- Collect the minimum needed.

## We will not build
- Paid placement or sponsored ranking.
- Advertising.
- Engagement mechanics for their own sake: streaks, artificial scarcity, manipulative notifications, infinite feeds.
- Dark patterns.
- Anything that sells or shares personal data.
- Closed or exclusive content.
- A closed-source fork of ourselves.

## Non-goals
- Not a social network. People may subscribe to roasters, cafés, and event hosts to hear about releases and events, but there is no engagement feed, and follower counts are never status. Community here means shared knowledge, not an audience.

## Amending this document
Changes should be proposed openly in the repository and recorded with their rationale. As the project grows, a `GOVERNANCE.md` may define a formal process.
